eBay Fees Explained: What You Actually Keep From a Sale

Most new sellers do the math like this: sold for $88, bought for $45, profit $43. Then the payout lands and it's nowhere near $43. Here's every line between the sale price and your pocket, worked out on a real example.

The fee stack

  • Final value fee: 13.6% for most categories. Some categories differ, but 13.6% is the rate the majority of resellers pay.
  • Per-order fee: $0.40 on orders over $10 ($0.30 at $10 or less), charged on every sale.
  • The fee applies to the total, not just the item price. If you charge the buyer $8 for shipping, the 13.6% comes out of price plus that shipping charge.
  • Promoted listings are extra. If a sale comes through a promoted listing, your ad rate (commonly 2–10%+) comes off the total too — only when it sells, but on top of everything above.
  • Your real costs ride along: what you paid for the item, what the shipping label actually costs you, and packaging. "Free shipping" doesn't remove these — it just moves them onto you.

Worked example: the $88 sale

An item sells for $88 with free shipping. You paid $45 for it, the label costs you $6.50, packaging $1.50, no promoted listing.

That's a 26% margin. Respectable, but it's $22.63, not $43. And it answers the question that actually matters before you buy: at a 30% target margin, the most you could have paid for this item was $41.23. Pay $45 for the next one at the same sale price and the deal quietly gets worse; pay $55 and you're working for free.

The three places sellers lose money without noticing

  • Many sellers guess the fee at 10%. It is 13.6% plus the per-order fee, and on an $88 sale, guessing 10% overstates your profit by more than $3, on every single sale.
  • Many sellers forget the fee hits shipping. Charge $10 shipping and eBay takes $1.36 of it before you've bought the label.
  • Many sellers never compute a max buy price. Profit is decided when you buy, not when you sell. A max-buy number, the ceiling that protects your target margin, turns "this feels like a deal" into a yes/no answer at the thrift store shelf.

Questions sellers ask about eBay fees

What is eBay's final value fee?

For most categories it is 13.6% of the total amount of the sale — the item price plus the shipping you charge — plus a per-order fee of $0.40 on orders over $10 ($0.30 on orders of $10 or less).

Does eBay charge its fee on shipping?

Yes. The final value fee is calculated on the total, including the shipping charged to the buyer. Charge $10 shipping and about $1.36 of it goes to the fee before you have bought the label.

What else comes out of an eBay sale besides the fee?

Your item cost, the shipping label, and packaging, plus a promoted-listing ad fee if you chose to promote. In our worked $88 example, the fee and costs total $65.37, leaving $22.63 of actual profit.

How do I know the most I should pay for an item to resell?

Work backward from your target margin: start with the expected sale price, subtract the fees and costs, and leave your margin. In this guide's example, a 30% target margin on an $88 sale puts the maximum buy price at $41.23.
