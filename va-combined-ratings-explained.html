How VA Combined Ratings Work: The Math VA Actually Uses

If you have a 50% rating and a 30% rating, the VA does not pay you at 80%. It pays you at 70% — and the reason is a formula most veterans have never seen written down. Here it is, with every step shown.

The whole-person idea

The VA rates disability against a whole, fully efficient person — 100%. Each condition removes a percentage of what remains, not a percentage of the whole. A 50% rating leaves you 50% "efficient." A second condition rated 30% takes 30% of that remaining 50% — 15 points — not 30 points off the top.

That's why VA ratings can never add past 100, and why stacking small ratings produces less than people expect. The official method is the Combined Ratings Table in 38 CFR §4.25.

The rules, in order

  1. Sort highest to lowest. Ratings are combined starting with the largest.
  2. Combine two at a time. Combined = A + B × (100 − A) ÷ 100. Repeat, feeding the result in as the new A.
  3. Bilateral conditions go first — and get a bonus. If conditions affect both sides of the body (both knees, both shoulders, an arm and a leg), combine those together first, then add 10% of that combined value back. This is the bilateral factor, and it's the step most online explanations skip.
  4. Round once, at the very end. The final number rounds to the nearest 10, with .5 rounding up. 76.78% becomes 80%. Nothing rounds along the way.

Worked example: 50% + 30% + 20% + 10%

Say the 30% and 20% are bilateral (both knees), and the 50% and 10% are not.

  • Bilateral pair first: 30 + 20 × (100 − 30) ÷ 100 = 30 + 14 = 44
  • Bilateral factor: 44 + (10% of 44) = 44 + 4.4 = 48.4
  • Combine with the 50%: 50 + 48.4 × (100 − 50) ÷ 100 = 50 + 24.2 = 74.2
  • Combine with the 10%: 74.2 + 10 × (100 − 74.2) ÷ 100 = 74.2 + 2.58 = 76.78
  • Round: 76.78% → 80% combined rating

Straight addition says 110%. VA math says 80%. At the current veteran-alone rates, 80% pays $2,102.15/month.

Notice the cliff between 90% and 100% — over $1,500 a month. That's why the rounding rule and the bilateral factor matter so much near the top: a single point of exact value can be the difference between rounding to 90 and rounding to 100.

Three mistakes that throw the number off

  • Adding ratings directly. The most common one — and the reason a "110%" claim pays at 80.
  • Rounding each step. VA rounds once, at the end. Rounding early can move the final answer by a full 10% bracket.
  • Forgetting the bilateral factor. If two or more of your rated conditions are bilateral, skipping the +10%-of-value step understates your rating — in the example above it was worth 4.4 points, which carried the final round-up to 80.
