The VA Bilateral Factor: The Extra Step Most Veterans Never Hear About

If you have rated conditions on both sides of your body — both knees, both shoulders, both feet — the VA owes your combined rating a bonus before it does anything else. It's called the bilateral factor, it's written into 38 CFR §4.26, and in the worked example below it was worth $293.70 a month by itself.

What "bilateral" means to the VA

The factor applies when you have compensable disabilities in both of a paired set of extremities: both upper extremities, both lower extremities, or paired skeletal muscle groups. Both knees rated, a shoulder on each side, both feet — those pair. A knee and a back do not: the back is midline, not one side of a pair. And "compensable" matters — a 0% service-connected rating doesn't count toward a pair.

The reasoning is practical: losing function on both sides costs you more than the sum of the two, because one side can't compensate for the other. The factor is how the regulation prices that.

The math, in three steps

  1. Combine the paired ratings first, using the normal combined-ratings method — each rating applies to what remains, never straight addition.
  2. Add 10% of that combined value. Not 10 points — 10 percent of the number from step 1. A paired value of 44 becomes 44 + 4.4 = 48.4.
  3. Treat the result as a single rating and combine it with everything else, highest first. Round once, at the very end, to the nearest 10 with .5 rounding up.

Worked example 1: just the pair — knees at 30% and 20%

  • Combine the pair: 30 + 20 × (100 − 30) ÷ 100 = 30 + 14 = 44
  • Bilateral factor: 44 + (10% of 44) = 44 + 4.4 = 48.4
  • Round: 48.4% → 50% combined rating

Without the factor, 44 rounds to 40%. The factor alone moved this veteran a full bracket — at the current veteran-alone rates, that's $1,132.90 a month instead of $795.84.

Worked example 2: the factor inside a full claim — 50% + 30% + 20% + 10%

The 30% and 20% are the bilateral knees; the 50% and 10% are not bilateral.

  • Pair, with factor: 44 + 4.4 = 48.4 (from example 1)
  • Combine with the 50%: 50 + 48.4 × (100 − 50) ÷ 100 = 50 + 24.2 = 74.2
  • Combine with the 10%: 74.2 + 10 × (100 − 74.2) ÷ 100 = 74.2 + 2.58 = 76.78
  • Round: 76.78% → 80% — $2,102.15/month

Now run the same claim without the factor: 44 combines with 50 to make 72, then with 10 to make 74.8 — which rounds to 70%, or $1,808.45/month. Same conditions, same ratings. The only difference is one line of §4.26, and it's worth $293.70 every month.

Four ways people get the factor wrong

  • Adding 10 points instead of 10 percent. The single most common error. The factor is 10% of the paired combined value — on a 44, that's 4.4 points, not 10.
  • Applying it to each rating separately. The factor applies once, to the combined value of the pair — never per condition.
  • Pairing things that don't pair. A left knee and a lower back are not a bilateral pair. Both sides of the same paired extremities (or paired muscle groups) is the test.
  • Counting a 0% rating. A 0% service-connected condition is noncompensable — it doesn't create a pair and doesn't trigger the factor.

Questions veterans ask about the bilateral factor

What is the VA bilateral factor?

When compensable disabilities affect paired extremities — both arms or both legs — the VA combines those ratings first, then adds 10% of that combined value before combining the result with your other ratings. The rule is 38 CFR §4.26.

Which disabilities qualify for the bilateral factor?

Compensable ratings on both sides of a pair: both knees, both shoulders, both feet, both elbows, and other paired extremities or paired skeletal muscle groups. One-sided conditions and midline conditions like the back or neck do not pair, so they never trigger the factor.

Is the bilateral factor 10 percentage points?

No. It is 10 percent of the combined value of the paired ratings, not 10 points added on top. A paired combined value of 44 gets +4.4, not +10.

Does a 0% rating trigger the bilateral factor?

No. The paired ratings must be compensable — 10% or higher. A 0% service-connected rating on one side does not create a bilateral pair.

When does the VA apply the bilateral factor?

Automatically, as part of the combined-ratings calculation, whenever your rated disabilities include compensable conditions on both sides of a pair. It is still worth checking the math in your decision letter — it is the easiest step to miss when verifying your rating.
